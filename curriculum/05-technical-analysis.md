# Module 5: Technical Analysis

Technical analysis (TA) studies price and volume history to inform trading
decisions. Where fundamentals ask "what is this business worth?", TA asks
"what is the crowd doing, and when should I act?"

**Honest framing up front:** the academic evidence for TA predicting prices is
weak, and most simple published patterns stop working once known. Treat TA as
a tool for *timing, discipline, and risk control* — not a crystal ball. Anyone
telling you a chart pattern "works 80% of the time" is selling something.

## Reading a chart

- **Candlestick**: each candle shows open, high, low, close for a period.
  Green/white = closed higher than it opened; red/black = closed lower.
- **Volume**: shares traded per period, shown as bars below the price. Moves
  on high volume mean more conviction than moves on low volume.
- **Timeframes**: daily charts for investors; weekly for the big picture.
  Minute charts are for day traders (i.e., mostly for losing money).

## Core concepts

### Trend

"The trend is your friend." An **uptrend** makes higher highs and higher lows;
a **downtrend** the reverse. The simplest use of TA that survives scrutiny:
avoid fighting a strong, established trend.

### Support and resistance

- **Support**: a price level where buying has repeatedly stopped declines.
- **Resistance**: a level where selling has repeatedly capped rallies.

These exist partly as self-fulfilling psychology — enough traders watch the
same levels that orders cluster there. A decisive **breakout** through
resistance on high volume is a classic bullish signal; former resistance often
becomes support.

### Moving averages (MA)

The average closing price over N days, redrawn daily — it smooths noise.

- **50-day MA**: medium-term trend.
- **200-day MA**: long-term trend. Price above a rising 200-day MA is the
  textbook definition of a healthy uptrend.
- **Golden cross** (50 crosses above 200) and **death cross** (opposite) are
  famous but lagging signals — they confirm trends late, not early.

## Common indicators

| Indicator | What it measures | Classic reading |
|-----------|------------------|-----------------|
| **RSI** (Relative Strength Index) | Speed of recent gains vs. losses, 0–100 | >70 "overbought," <30 "oversold" — but strong trends stay overbought for months |
| **MACD** | Gap between two exponential MAs | Crossovers signal momentum shifts |
| **Bollinger Bands** | Volatility envelope around a 20-day MA | Price hugging a band = strong trend; band squeeze = big move brewing |
| **VWAP** | Volume-weighted average price (intraday) | Institutional benchmark for "fair" intraday price |

Every indicator is computed from past prices. None contains information about
the future that the price itself doesn't. Their value is giving you
*consistent, rule-based* reasons to act, replacing impulse.

## Chart patterns (and appropriate skepticism)

You'll hear about head-and-shoulders, double tops/bottoms, cup-and-handle,
flags, triangles. Know them so you understand what other traders are seeing.
Backtests show weak or no reliable edge for most patterns; humans are
pattern-matching machines who find shapes in clouds too.

## What TA is actually good for

1. **Entry/exit discipline** — "buy the breakout above resistance with a stop
   below" is a *plan*; "buy because it feels low" is a mood.
2. **Risk placement** — support levels give logical places for stop-losses.
3. **Avoiding falling knives** — waiting for a downtrend to stabilize before
   buying a fundamentally cheap stock.
4. **Position awareness** — knowing whether you're buying into strength or
   weakness, and sizing accordingly.

A sensible synthesis used by many pros: **fundamentals decide *what* to buy,
technicals help decide *when*.**

## What TA cannot do

- Predict news, earnings surprises, or crashes.
- Overcome the costs and taxes of frequent trading.
- Turn a small account into a fortune quickly (anyone claiming this — run).
